10. What is aphasia?

facts ✓grade 2.6education-aphasia-en-001 · en · education · made-up (written for this project)
Original
Aphasia: Information for Patients and Families. Aphasia is an acquired language disorder caused by damage to the language areas of the brain, most often from a stroke. It can affect speaking, understanding, reading, and writing. Aphasia does not affect intelligence. People with aphasia know what they want to say but may have trouble finding the words. Speech-language therapy can improve communication, and many people continue to improve for years. Communication partners can help by speaking slowly, using short sentences, writing key words, and allowing extra time. For more information, contact Piedmont Speech Clinic at (919) 555-0107.

facts ✓grade 2.5mental-health-anxiety-en-001 · en · mental_health · made-up (written for this project)
Original
Managing Anxiety After a Stroke. Anxiety, such as feeling worried, nervous, or panicky, is common after a stroke. Strategies that may help: slow breathing (breathe in for 4 counts, out for 6 counts), regular physical activity as approved by your doctor, keeping a daily routine, and talking with people you trust. Tell your doctor if anxiety makes it hard to sleep, eat, or do daily activities. Counseling is available through Oak Valley Counseling Center; call (919) 555-0108. If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, call or text 988, the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline.

23. ¿Qué es la afasia?

facts ✓grade 1.2education-aphasia-es-001 · es · education · made-up (written for this project)
Original
Afasia: información para pacientes y familias. La afasia es un trastorno adquirido del lenguaje causado por daño en las áreas del lenguaje del cerebro, casi siempre por un derrame cerebral. Puede afectar el habla, la comprensión, la lectura y la escritura. La afasia no afecta la inteligencia. Las personas con afasia saben lo que quieren decir, pero les puede costar encontrar las palabras. La terapia del habla puede mejorar la comunicación, y muchas personas siguen mejorando por años. La familia puede ayudar si habla despacio, usa frases cortas, escribe palabras clave y da más tiempo. Para más información, llame a la Clínica del Habla Piedmont al (919) 555-0117.
